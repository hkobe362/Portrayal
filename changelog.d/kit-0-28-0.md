### Changed
- **`@portrayal/kit` is 0.28.0: a bundle's straps are measured and placed on
  its cables** (#1034, `kit/rack/bundles.js`). `straps(rack, bundle, ctx)`
  and `trunkLength` measured a trunk along the straight line from one
  waypoint to the next. Its members do not run there: a cord passes a ring
  by its opening, hangs between a ring and a lane, and goes round a body by
  a detour point. So a trunk was measured short and a strap's `segment` and
  `t` named a place off the cables. Both now read the members' routed paths.
  **What changes for a rack that is drawn today:** `runs` and each strap's
  `along_mm` and `t`; the count of spacing straps, and with it `count`,
  `kinds`, `widths` and `strapTotals`; `trunkLength`, a bundle's `length`
  and `straps` in `inspect`, and `length_m`, `straps`, `strap_kinds` and
  `strap_widths` in `bundleExports`; so the bundle's line on the sheet and
  in the cable schedule, and the quantity and widths of the BOM's strap
  line. A trunk that only runs along a lane is measured as it was. One that
  passes a ring, a duct or a tray, or goes from one lane to another, is as
  long as its cords, and takes one more strap where that crosses a multiple
  of the spacing. On the rack of `spec/tests/js/strap-path-fixture.mjs` (96
  cords in eight bundles, four from a rear lane to a front one) the strap
  line goes from 54 to 58, and the two bundles that go round a duct from
  1.15 m and 6 straps to 1.63 m and 8.
- **The strap record gains three keys**: `point` (`{x, y, z}` in rack
  coordinates, mm), `dir` (the way the trunk runs there, a unit vector, or
  null) and `on` (the id of the cable whose path the point is on, null where
  no member's path could be read and the straight line stands in). `segment`,
  `t` and `along_mm` stay, and `t` is the share of the segment's routed
  length. A reader that compared a strap record key for key sees the new
  keys.
- Where members differ, each piece of the trunk (between two pathways, or two
  units of a lane) is taken from the member whose path is longest there, a
  tie to the id that sorts first by number; the order cables or members are
  written in plays no part. A place half way between two tenths of a
  millimetre now always rounds up, where it rounded by the last bit of a
  sum: one run end and one strap on three recorded racks read a tenth of a
  millimetre more.
- Not changed: which tie slot an anchor is at, the strap widths, a bundle's
  bend check (its corners are still the straight lines between waypoints), a
  single cable's path, length and stock size, and every saved rack: none of
  this is stored. Nothing is added to what the kit keeps of a lay; reading
  every bundle's straps on the generated rack of 300 cords takes about 4 ms
  where it took 2.
