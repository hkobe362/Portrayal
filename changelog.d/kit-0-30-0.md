### Added
- **`@portrayal/kit` is 0.30.0: a power cord, a DC feed and a ground wire can
  land in a rack** (#1045, `docs/cable-ends-design.md`). A cable landed only on
  a part of class `port`, and no power end of the library is one. The rule is
  now one function, `cableLandsOn` in `kit/rack/cable-rules.js`: a part of
  class `port`, or a connector slot that accepts at least one part of class
  `port` (a cord end or a lug). Over the built catalogue that admits 305
  slots presented by parts of class `inlet`, 127 by `ground`, 8 by the screws
  of DC terminals and 6 by placed supplies, and refuses every pluggable cage,
  every module bay and the one guard seat. The address of such an end is the
  path of its slot (`psu-1/module/inlet`, `outlet-rear-3`, `ground`). No
  inlet, outlet or device changes, and no class.
- **Two new values of a cable's `media`, `power` and `ground`**, in the rack
  file, the published rack schema's description of `media`, and the
  `cable.add` and `cable.update` commands. `power` is an AC cord or a DC feed;
  `ground` is a bonding wire. Each has a diameter, a colour, a plug reach and
  an end allowance; the ground figures and the cord's length tolerance are
  estimates and say so.
- **A new optional key on a cable end, `plug`**: the plug or lug the cable has
  there, a component ref, stated only where the slot takes more than one.
  With none stated the plug is derived from what the slot accepts, the lowest
  rated first. A derived plug is never a swap, a bill-of-materials line or an
  export row.
- New exports of `rack/cable-rules.js`: `cableLandsOn`, `takesCableEnd`,
  `cableEndsOf`, `endPlug`, `receptacleOf`, `endKind`, `connectorName`. Of
  `rack/ports.js`: `ownSlot`, `cableEndPaths`, `shownEnv`. `plugSeats` takes a fourth
  argument, `freePorts` a fourth (`{kinds}`), `readSvg` an `ends` option and
  `readDrawing` a third argument.

### Changed
- **Answers that change for a rack with such a cable.** `looseEnds`, `inspect`,
  the cable schedule, the cable notes of every export and the DCIM cables file
  read an end on a supply inlet, a DC terminal screw, a PDU outlet with an
  interface or a ground stud as connected, where each read it as loose. A
  rack with no cable on such a part reads exactly as it did.
- `looseReason` has two new sentences: a part that takes no cable end is named
  ("outlet-9 is NEMA 5-20R, which takes no plug or lug the library knows";
  "psu-1 holds PSU-AC, whose led takes no plug or lug the library knows",
  which read "which has no port led"), and a slot on a side, top or bottom
  view says an end can be given on the front or rear only. An end that names
  a DC terminal and no screw is told which path to give
  ("...takes a cable on lug-1, lug-2, lug-3 and not on itself; give the path
  psu-1/module/terminal/lug-1").
- `mismatch` knows the families `power` and `ground`, and says an AC end
  against a DC end and two outlets or two inlets where both ends state it.
  It still refuses nothing.
- `proposeMedia` and `suggestMedia` offer `power` or `ground` where the first
  end that states a family is one. `MEDIA` has ten values; the device
  schema's `cable-capacity-media` keeps its eight.
- `freePorts` lists the slots a cable lands on where the page hands them in
  (`readSvg` with `ends`), each with `kind`, `connector` and `plug`. A page
  that does not ask gets the list it got, entry for entry.
- The bill of materials and the cable schedule name a power cable and a ground
  wire ("Power cable", "2 m; power at both ends"). The DCIM cables file
  leaves a ground wire out by name; a power cable is written with a blank
  cable type and a note where the type files name both its ends, and left out
  with the reason where they do not.
- `cable.update` with an end named at the port it is on and another `plug`
  edits the cable; it was read as no change.
